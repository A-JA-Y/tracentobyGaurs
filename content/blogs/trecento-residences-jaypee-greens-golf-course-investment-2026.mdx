## The Scarcity Thesis

<a id="scarcity"></a>

Start with the arithmetic, because it is the whole argument. A typical NCR luxury tower holds somewhere between 300 and 800 apartments. Assume a normal churn rate — say three to five per cent of owners want to sell in any given year for the usual reasons: a job move, a divorce, an inheritance, a portfolio rebalance. In a 500-unit building that is fifteen to twenty-five units on the market at once, all broadly identical, all competing on price. The seller with the most urgent reason to sell sets the benchmark for everyone else.

Trecento holds sixty. The same churn rate produces two or three sellers a year, and because floor level and orientation vary meaningfully across a 30-storey tower, those two or three homes are not perfect substitutes for each other. Thin resale supply here is structural rather than cyclical. It does not depend on the market being good.

<PullQuote>
  "Sixty homes is the entire supply, permanently. Most NCR luxury towers hold 300 to 800 units — which is why their resale markets behave completely differently."
</PullQuote>

This matters most in a downturn. Scarcity does not prevent prices falling, but it changes the mechanism: in a large tower, the marginal distressed seller drags the whole building's benchmark down, and every valuation in the building follows. In a sixty-unit building, one distressed sale is one data point in a market too thin for anyone to treat as the benchmark.

## The Golf-Course Premium

<a id="golf"></a>

Views are the most over-sold and under-analysed attribute in Indian residential real estate. The question that actually matters is not how good the view is today, but whether anyone can take it away from you.

The tower stands in the middle of Jaypee Greens' 18-hole international course, designed by Greg Norman and among the longest in the world, inside a 182.92-hectare (452-acre) township developed and managed by Jaiprakash Associates Ltd. A golf course is not vacant land awaiting a better use — it is a built, maintained, contractually committed piece of township infrastructure. That is a fundamentally different proposition from a view over an empty plot that will eventually hold someone else's tower.

<Callout type="warning">
  Public infrastructure timelines slip, routinely and substantially. Treat every item above as directional. An investment case that requires all of them to land on schedule is not an investment case — it is a hope. The ones that are already operational, like the expressways and the Aqua Line, are worth more to your analysis than the ones that are proposed.
</Callout>

## The Case Against

<a id="risks"></a>

Any analysis that only argues one direction is marketing. Here is what a buyer should weigh against everything above.

**Delivery risk is real.** July 2028 is a proposed date on an under-construction project. The developer's record reduces this risk; it does not eliminate it. Structure your payment plan so that your exposure tracks actual construction progress, and check the RERA filing for the committed completion date rather than relying on the brochure.

**Scarcity cuts both ways.** The same thin market that protects pricing on the way up makes exit slower. In a sixty-unit building there may be no comparable sale for months, which makes your asset hard to value, hard to finance for the next buyer, and hard to liquidate quickly. If there is any chance you need this capital back inside five years, this is the wrong asset.

**Price opacity puts the work on you.** Pricing is on request. That is defensible given the variation across floors and orientations, but it means you cannot benchmark from a web page. Get quotes on comparable per-sq.-ft. rates elsewhere in the corridor before you sign.

**Township facilities have terms.** Several Jaypee Greens facilities — including elements of the golf and club infrastructure — are members-only and/or chargeable. Confirm in writing exactly what comes with the home, at what recurring cost. This is the most common source of post-purchase disappointment in golf townships anywhere.

**Recurring costs scale with size.** A 5000+ sq. ft. home carries maintenance charges to match, indefinitely. Include them in your yield arithmetic, not just your purchase arithmetic.
